中文卷：灵息杯第一届正赛（中文卷）

Conventions. Throughout, $\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}$ — following the convention used in Chinese textbooks, the natural numbers include $0$, so $0\in\mathbb{N}$ — and $\mathbb{Z}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$; for a subfield $K\subseteq\mathbb{C}$ and a set $S\subseteq\mathbb{C}$, $K(S)$ denotes the smallest subfield of $\mathbb{C}$ containing $K$ and $S$; a polynomial is called irreducible over $K$ only if it is non-constant.

Problem 1 (20 points)

Prove that for all $n,m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1\}$ (i.e. $n,m\ge2$) there exist $x,y,z\in\mathbb{N}$ with

$$x^n+y^n \equiv z^n \pmod{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad z^n \not\equiv 0 \pmod{m}$$

($x,y,z$ are required to be pairwise distinct. Recall $0\in\mathbb{N}$, so each of them is allowed to be $0$.)

Problem 2 (20 points)

Let $$f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n a_i x^i\ \Big(\sum_{j=1}^n a_j^2\neq 0\Big)\in\mathbb{Z}[x].$$ Prove that for every constant $C\in\mathbb{R}$,

$${\lvert\{u \in \mathbb{R}\mid f(u) = C\}\rvert\leq n}$$

Problem 3 (20 points)

Let $$K_0=\mathbb{Q},\qquad G_i=\{p\in\mathbb{C}\mid p^o=q^r,\ q\in K_i,\ r,o\in\mathbb{Z}^*\},\qquad K_j=K_{j-1}(G_{j-1}),$$ and let $f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ be non-constant, $n=\deg f$.

  1. (2 points) Is it true that ($f$ irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$) $\implies$ ($f$ irreducible over $K_1$)? Answer the question and prove your answer.
  2. (6 points) Is it true that ($f$ irreducible over $K_1$) $\implies$ ($f$ irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$)? Answer the question and prove your answer.
  3. (6 points) Is it true that $(\exists M\in\mathbb{N})(\forall f\in\mathbb{Z}[x])[(f\ \text{irreducible over}\ K_M)\implies(f\ \text{irreducible over}\ \mathbb{R})]$? (Recall $0\in\mathbb{N}$, so $M=0$ is allowed and then $K_M=K_0=\mathbb{Q}$.) Determine, in terms of the degree $n=\deg f$, for which $n$ it holds and for which it fails, and prove your answer.
  4. (6 points) Let $$K^*_0=\mathbb{Q},\qquad G^*_i=\{p\in\mathbb{R}\mid p^o=q^r,\ q\in K^*_i,\ r,o\in\mathbb{Z}^*\},\qquad K^*_j=K^*_{j-1}(G^*_{j-1}).$$ Find $f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ that is irreducible over $K^*_M$ for every $M\in\mathbb{N}$ but reducible over $\mathbb{R}$, and factor it over $\mathbb{R}$.

Notes

(i) Every polynomial equation of degree $\le4$ is solvable by radicals (quadratic formula, Cardano, Ferrari).

(ii) (Abel–Ruffini) There is no general formula in radicals for equations of degree $\ge5$; e.g. no root of $x^5-4x+2$ is expressible by radicals. More generally, for every $n\ge5$, $x^n-x-1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ and remains so over every field obtained from $\mathbb{Q}$ by iterated adjunction of radicals.

(iii) (Casus irreducibilis) If an irreducible cubic over $\mathbb{Q}$ has three real roots, then none of its roots can be expressed by real radicals, however deeply nested.

(iv) If $g\in K_j[x]$ has degree $\le4$, then by (i) every root of $g$ lies in some $K_{j'}$ ($j'\ge j$). Hence if $f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ has a factor of degree $\le4$ over some $K_j$, then $f$ has a root expressible by radicals.

Problem 4 (20 points)

The eight moves of an ordinary chess knight are

$$K_2=\{\pm1\pm 2i,\ \pm 2 \pm i\}\subset\mathbb{Z}[i]$$

Define the $a$-knight (strange A-knight) by

$$K_a=\{\pm1\pm ai,\ \pm a \pm i\}\subset\mathbb{Z}[i]$$

(where $\pm1\pm ai$ stands for all four independent choices of the two signs).

  1. (6 points) Prove that if $a\equiv1\pmod2$, then $\sum_{i=1}^n k_i\neq 1$ for every $n\ge1$ and all $k_1,\ldots,k_n\in K_a$.
    $$\sum_{i=1}^n k_i\neq 1$$
  2. (6 points) Prove that if $a\equiv0\pmod2$, then for every $z\in\mathbb{Z}[i]$ there exist $n\ge1$ and $k_1,\ldots,k_n\in K_a$ with $$\sum_{i=1}^n k_i=z$$
  3. (8 points) Let $m\ge5$ be an integer and let $B_m=\{a+bi\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\mid 0\le a,b<m\}$ be the $m\times m$ board. Prove that for every $z\in B_m$ there exist $n\ge1$ and $k_1,\ldots,k_n\in K_2$ such that $$\sum_{i=1}^n k_i=z\qquad\text{and}\qquad \sum_{i=1}^j k_i\in B_m\ \ (1\le j\le n),$$ i.e. an ordinary knight starting at the corner $0$ can reach every square by a path that never leaves the board.
Problem 5 (20 points)

Let $n\ge1$ and let $K=(a_1,\ldots ,a_n)$ be a finite sequence with $a_i\in\{0,1\}$. Define $T(K)=(b_1,\ldots ,b_n)$ as follows.

Step 1 (instructions). Each position $i$ issues at most one instruction.

  • If $a_i=1$, put $p_i=\bigl|\{j<i\mid a_j=1\}\bigr|$, the number of $1$'s standing before it. If $p_i\ge2$, position $i$ issues the instruction “put a $1$ into position $i+p_i-1$”.
  • If $a_i=0$, put $s_i=\bigl|\{j>i\mid a_j=0\}\bigr|$, the number of $0$'s standing after it. If $s_i\ge2$, position $i$ issues the instruction “put a $0$ into position $i-s_i+1$”.

In all other cases no instruction is issued: if the number counted is $0$ or $1$ (counting begins at the position itself, so the first place from $a_i$ is $a_i$ itself), or if the indicated position is outside $\{1,\ldots ,n\}$ (no wrap-around, no overflow).

Step 2 (simultaneous execution). All instructions are computed from $K$ and carried out at the same time. If position $t$ receives only “$1$” instructions, put $b_t=1$; if only “$0$” instructions, put $b_t=0$; if it receives both kinds, or no instruction at all, put $b_t=a_t$.

Question. Prove that $T(K)$ always has a fixed point: there exists $t\in\{1,\ldots ,n\}$ with $b_t=a_t$.
